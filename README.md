## Case study brief

Example Foundation implements the Community Facilitation Model (CFM), a community-driven development (CDD) model, in rural communities in Ghana in partnership with Partner Organisation (PartnerOrg). The programme currently operates in 100 communities, with plans to scale to 200 communities across the Northern and Southern regions in 2026.

In each participating village, all households are encouraged to take part in CFM meetings, where they go through a six-month planning phase before receiving a seed grant to implement a community-selected project. Example Foundation and its partners then provide ongoing support and supervision for up to 18 months during project implementation, after which communities graduate from the CFM process.

The Ghana team has asked the M&E Officer to design an M&E plan for the new communities, including learning questions, indicators, and key decision points.

**Tasks**

1. Outline what could be included in an M&E plan and the process to design it with the Ghana team, including any questions to answer.
2. For the learning question *"To what extent does the CFM lead to better community planning, cohesion, and project implementation outcomes?"*, describe the indicators to track, how often, and through which channel.
3. Describe how the M&E Officer would interact with and support the Ghana team once the plan is designed.

## Assumptions

- The 200 new communities span the Northern and Southern regions, with heterogeneous contexts.
- No pre-existing M&E framework exists for the scale-up.
- Data collection will involve both Example Foundation staff and PartnerOrg field officers.
- Communities are at similar starting points (pre-CFM), and household-level data collection is feasible with trained enumerators.

## 1. M&E plan outline and design process

### Purpose

The M&E plan serves three functions:

- **Tracking fidelity:** are activities implemented as designed?
- **Measuring outcomes:** are communities benefiting?
- **Generating learning:** what can be improved and applied to future scale?

### Plan structure

The plan has five interconnected components:

1. Programme Theory of Change (ToC) and Results Framework
2. Learning Questions and Indicators
3. Data Collection Plan (tools, frequency, method, sources, responsibility)
4. Data Management, Quality Assurance, and Analysis
5. Reporting, Learning, and Adaptive Management

### Theory of Change and Results Framework

The plan is anchored in a validated ToC that maps the causal pathway:

| Level | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Inputs and activities | CFM facilitation, seed grant, community meetings |
| Outputs | Households participating, meetings held, projects initiated |
| Outcomes | Improved planning, cohesion, livelihoods |
| Impact | Reduced poverty, strengthened community agency |

A logframe or results matrix will be developed with Example Foundation and PartnerOrg to give each level measurable indicators.

### Data collection plan

The plan uses a mixed-methods design.

- **Quantitative:** structured household surveys at baseline (before CFM begins), midline (month 9), and endline (month 24 post-enrolment).
- **Qualitative:** focus group discussions (FGDs), key informant interviews (KIIs), and community observation checklists at the same intervals, plus quarterly process monitoring visits.

| Data type | Tool / method | Frequency | Responsible | Coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Household survey | Structured questionnaire (KoboToolbox) | Baseline / midline / endline | PartnerOrg enumerators + Example Foundation M&E | 30% household sample per village |
| Process monitoring | Attendance registers, field visit checklist | Monthly | PartnerOrg field officers | All 200 communities |
| Community-level data | Village assessment tool (VAT) | Quarterly | Example Foundation M&E Officer | All 200 communities |
| FGDs | 4–6 FGDs per region per wave | Midline & endline | Example Foundation M&E + PartnerOrg | Purposive sample (20 villages) |
| KIIs | Village leaders, women's group reps, project committee chairs | Annually | Example Foundation M&E Officer | 10% of communities |
| Grievance / feedback | Community feedback forms, toll-free line | Ongoing | PartnerOrg Community Liaison | All communities |

### Data management and quality assurance

- Data is collected via KoboToolbox, with skip logic and validation rules to minimise entry errors.
- Double data entry verification and supervisor spot-checks are run on submissions to the database.
- Data is stored in a centralised repository with role-based access controls.
- Monthly data quality review meetings assess completeness, consistency, and reliability.

### Reporting and adaptive management

| Product | Frequency | Audience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dashboards | Monthly | Ghana team |
| Learning briefs (findings and recommendations) | Quarterly | Example Foundation leadership, PartnerOrg management |
| Reflection workshops | Bi-annual | Community representatives, field staff |
| Evaluation report (with simplified community summaries) | Annual | Example Foundation HQ, donors, communities |

Adaptive management runs on pre-defined decision thresholds. For example, a review and corrective action is triggered if attendance at community meetings falls below **40% for two consecutive months**.

### Design process with the Ghana team

The plan is co-designed with the Ghana team to ensure contextual relevance and ownership.

| Phase | Activity | Who is involved | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Discovery (weeks 1–2) | Review existing reports, PartnerOrg evaluations, and programme documents; map the data landscape | M&E Officer | Annotated document review; gap analysis |
| 2. Inception workshops (weeks 3–4) | 2-day co-design workshop with Example Foundation Ghana and PartnerOrg to validate the ToC, agree learning questions, and assign data ownership | Example Foundation Ghana PM, M&E Officer, PartnerOrg M&E Lead, 2–3 field officers | Draft results framework; agreed learning questions |
| 3. Indicator selection (week 5) | Define each indicator: definition, source, baseline, target, disaggregation, frequency; validate against the ToC | M&E Officer + Example Foundation technical team | Indicator tracking table (MEAL matrix) |
| 4. Tool development (weeks 5–6) | Adapt or design household survey and observation checklists; pre-test in 2–3 communities | PartnerOrg data team + M&E Officer | Piloted, validated tools in KoboToolbox |
| 5. Baseline survey (month 2) | Train enumerators, collect baseline data, clean and analyse | PartnerOrg enumerators, Example Foundation M&E Officer | Baseline report; baseline values for all indicators |
| 6. Plan finalisation (months 2–3) | Finalise the plan with baseline findings; submit to Example Foundation HQ for approval | M&E Officer, Example Foundation Ghana PM | Approved M&E plan |

### Questions to answer before finalising

- What are the budget and staffing for M&E in the scale-up, and will PartnerOrg have a dedicated data officer?
- How will the plan align with existing evaluation frameworks and indicators from the current 100 communities?
- What do donors and Example Foundation HQ require in terms of reporting frequency, format, and indicators?
- How will the target population be disaggregated (gender, age cohort, household wealth)?
- Is baseline data already available for the 200 new communities, or is a full baseline survey needed?
- What mechanisms for community feedback and accountability already exist within CFM?
- How will M&E findings feed into PartnerOrg's adaptive programme management, and is there a formal learning cycle?

## 2. Indicators for the learning question

> **To what extent does the CFM lead to better community planning, cohesion, and project implementation outcomes?**

### Community planning

| Indicator | Definition / measurement | Frequency | Channel | Disaggregation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **% of households actively participating in planning meetings** | HHs attending ≥ 3 of 6 CFM planning sessions ÷ total HHs × 100 | Each 6-month planning cycle | Attendance registers (PartnerOrg field officers) | Gender of HH representative; wealth quintile |
| **Quality of community action plan (CAP)** | Rubric score (0–10): specificity of activities, budget, roles assigned, timeline, community endorsement | At CAP finalisation (month 6) | Standardised plan review form (Example Foundation M&E Officer) | Northern vs Southern region |
| **% of priorities identified by women and youth reflected in final CAP** | Women/youth-identified priorities in final plan ÷ total women/youth-identified priorities × 100 | At CAP finalisation | FGDs with women and youth groups; plan document review | Gender; age group (15–29, 30+) |
| **Community satisfaction with planning process** | % rating the process "good" or "very good" on a 5-point Likert scale | End of planning phase (month 6) | Short participant survey (paper or KoboToolbox) | Gender; first-time vs returning communities |

### Community cohesion

| Indicator | Definition / measurement | Frequency | Channel | Disaggregation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Social cohesion index score** | Composite of 5 survey items adapted from the Social Capital Assessment Tool: trust in neighbours, willingness to cooperate, collective conflict resolution, inclusivity, sense of belonging. Scored 0–100. | Baseline, midline, endline | Household survey (KoboToolbox) | Gender; region; length of residence |
| **% reporting increased trust in village leadership** | % agreeing that "Village leaders make decisions that reflect the views of all community members" | Midline & endline | Household survey | Gender; wealth quintile |
| **Collective community actions beyond CFM-mandated activities** | Count of self-initiated activities (e.g. voluntary clean-ups, contributions to schools) per quarter | Quarterly | Field observation checklist; KIIs with community leaders | Type of action; CFM-prompted vs spontaneous |
| **Inclusion of marginalised groups in village meetings** | % of meetings where persons with disabilities, women, and youth are observed participating actively (speaking, voting) | Monthly during implementation | PartnerOrg field officer observation form | Women; youth; persons with disabilities |

### Project implementation

| Indicator | Definition / measurement | Frequency | Channel | Disaggregation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **% of projects completed on time and within budget** | Projects reaching ≥ 90% completion by planned end date ÷ total projects × 100; budget variance tracked | Quarterly (endline reconciliation) | Project monitoring form; PartnerOrg financial reporting | Region; project type |
| **Community project satisfaction rate** | % of HHs rating the project as addressing a priority need and well executed (5-point scale) | Endline (month 24) | Household survey + FGDs | Gender; proximity to project site |
| **% of seed grant funds utilised appropriately** | Grant expenditure verified against budget ÷ total grant × 100; flags for unaccounted funds | Quarterly financial review | PartnerOrg financial reports; Example Foundation spot audits | Project type; community size |
| **Project committee functionality score** | Rubric (0–10): regular meetings, records kept, complaints resolved, transparent reporting to community | Every 3 months during implementation | Example Foundation M&E field visit; KII with committee chair | Committee gender composition |
| **Maintenance plan in place at graduation** | Yes/No: approved, documented plan for ongoing project maintenance after graduation | At graduation (month 24) | Document review + KII | Region; project type |

## 3. Supporting the Ghana team during implementation

Once the plan is approved, the M&E Officer's role shifts from designer to implementer and enabler: making sure the Ghana team has the capacity, tools, systems, and motivation to collect high-quality data, use it for adaptive management, and build a culture of evidence-based learning.

### Capacity building and training

- Enumerator training (3–4 days) on survey navigation, interview technique, ethics, and KoboToolbox.
- Training for PartnerOrg M&E staff on data cleaning and statistical literacy.
- Field officer training on completing checklists and handling data anomalies.
- Quarterly refresher sessions over Zoom.
- Peer-learning exchange visits between high- and low-performing communities.

### Supervision, mentorship, and technical support

| Cadence | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Weekly | Calls to resolve data issues |
| Monthly | Data reviews to discuss observations |
| Quarterly | Field visits to validate data quality and coach teams |
| Bi-annual | Reflection and adaptation workshops |
| Annual | Evaluation to share findings and update priorities |

### Data use and learning culture

- Monthly dashboard for field teams.
- A "Data Champions" system for data quality and peer sharing.
- Community feedback sessions with local-language translation.
- Concise monthly "learning notes".
- Bi-annual workshops to adapt the programme based on data.

### Data quality assurance

- Weekly automated KoboToolbox checks for missing data and discrepancies.
- Monthly 10% back-checks of questionnaires by PartnerOrg supervisors.
- Quarterly inter-rater reliability assessment for qualitative data.
- Sharing anonymised aggregate data with communities to validate findings and catch errors.

### Adaptive management and scale-up support

- A monthly community-level tracking sheet to identify at-risk communities.
- A decision-rule matrix that triggers reviews.
- Advocacy for a staggered roll-out design to support learning.
- Support for PartnerOrg to institutionalise M&E systems beyond Example Foundation's involvement.
